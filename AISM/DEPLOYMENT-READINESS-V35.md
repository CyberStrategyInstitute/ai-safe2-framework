# AISM and deployment readiness in v3.5

The organizational AISM v1 formula and its historical reports retain their meanings. Deployment readiness is a separate, scoped evidence review. A strong organizational average cannot compensate for missing applicable authority, stopping, outcome verification or other mandatory evidence. The accountable owner identifies the required control set based on consequence and applicable obligations.

Report maturity, implementation, effectiveness, assurance and deployment authorization as separate outcomes. The new readiness evaluator returns hold or ready-for-human-evidence-review. It grants no authority. Its observations, including claimed independence, remain declarations until independently authenticated and assessed. A historical score cannot grant permission for a changed deployment.

Use `safe2 framework readiness evidence.json` with `deployment-readiness-v1`. Required control IDs must exist. Every required control needs exactly one current observation matching the revision and scope. Missing, failed, untested, self-reported, stale, future-dated or conflicting observations hold the result. The owner must verify the applicability inventory itself; a caller-selected narrow list cannot establish comprehensive deployment readiness.

The schema is discoverable through `safe2 schema export deployment-readiness-v1`. Record the exact system composition, policy, environment, evidence locations, exceptions and revisit triggers alongside the input. Protect privacy through opaque evidence IDs and controlled evidence stores. Scope approvals separately from evidence checks.

## Threat profiles

The eleven existing threat families remain stable. Three explicit cross-family profiles deepen assurance/oversight compromise, unsafe authorized execution and human-agent trust exploitation. See [the profile data](../safe2/data/threat-profiles-v3.5.json) and [observed-source register](../safe2/data/threat-observations-v3.5.json). These are linked failure mechanisms, with separate actor/origin, surface, authority, persistence, effect and recovery fields.

## Comparison and interpretation

Use informative relationships for external maturity or risk schemes. External tiers have no automatic AISM equivalence. A CVSS/maturity multiplication remains a CSI heuristic unless calibrated for the stated use. Research E0-E5, CLI numeric grades, Evidence Assurance, rubric A-D and Challenge Lab are separate instruments. Show the owning scale and denominator instead of an implicit conversion.
